\title{The $N$-Nanowire Qubit with Vortices:\\[2pt]
Asymmetric Wire Positions and the Likharev Current--Phase Relation}
\author{Shashank Satish}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We derive, from the very first step, the current--phase relation (CPR) and the Hamiltonian
of a superconducting device made of $N$ nanowires connected in parallel between two
electrodes, with the wires at \emph{arbitrary} (asymmetric) positions, a magnetic field
through the loops, an arbitrary integer number of vortices $n_k$ in each of the $N-1$
loops, and the cubic Likharev CPR for every wire.  The result has the form
$I/I_0=A\varphi-D\varphi^{3}-R$ and
$\hat H=-4E_C\,\partial_\varphi^{2}+\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}\bigl(\tfrac12A\varphi^{2}-\tfrac14D\varphi^{4}-R\varphi\bigr)$,
where $A$, $D$, $R$ are written out explicitly in terms of the wire positions $x_i$, the
field $b$ and the vortex numbers $n_k$.  Every algebraic step, including the most basic ones,
is written down.  Three parity checks are placed at the places where they matter most.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conventions and notation}
% ===========================================================================
We will calculate the CPR and the Hamiltonian for the $N$-nanowire device with vortices,
asymmetrical, and using the Likharev CPR.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l p{0.80\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$N\ge 2$ & number of nanowires (so there are $N-1$ loops) \\
$i$ & wire label, $i=1,\dots,N$ (the \emph{free} label in $\varphi_i$) \\
$x_i$ & position of wire $i$ along the electrode, in units of the outer separation $W$; $x_1<x_2<\dots<x_N$ \\
$b$ & normalized magnetic field, $b=BLW/\Phi_0$ (flux through a loop of width $W$ in units of the flux quantum $\Phi_0=h/2e$) \\
$n_k$ & vortex (winding) number of loop $k$, the loop between wire $k$ and wire $k+1$, $k=1,\dots,N-1$ \\
$\varphi_i$ & continuous phase drop along wire $i$ \\
$\varphi$ & characteristic phase, $\varphi=\frac1N\sum_i\varphi_i$ (the one free phase variable) \\
$\varphi_0,\ I_0$ & Likharev phase scale and current scale: $I_i=I_0\bigl[\varphi_i/\varphi_0-(\varphi_i/\varphi_0)^3\bigr]$ \\
$E_C$ & charging energy, $E_C=e^{2}/2C$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Dummy indices.}  A summation index may be called anything.  To avoid clashes when
several sums sit inside one expression we use fixed letters: $i$ is the wire label;
$k$ is used for sums over positions $\sum_k x_k$; $p$ for the weighted vorticity sum
$\sum_p (N-p)n_p$; and $j,l,q$ for the partial sums of vortex numbers.
(In the Meissner and characteristic-phase sections the loop index is also called $k$,
as in the notes; when it is merged into the final $\varphi_i$ formula (Section~\ref{sec:phii}) it is renamed.)

\textbf{Boxes.}  A framed box without a title is a result; a framed box titled ``Parity check'' is a check;
grey boxes are remarks.

\section{Geometry of the device}
% ===========================================================================
Two bulk superconducting electrodes (top and bottom) are connected by $N$ straight nanowires
of length $L$ that run parallel to the $y$-axis (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}).  The wire $i$ sits
at the position $x_i$ along the $x$-axis.  We measure $x$ in units of the outer separation
$W$ between the first and last wire and put the origin in the middle, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
x_1=-\tfrac12,\qquad x_N=+\tfrac12,\qquad x_N-x_1=1 .
\end{equation}
The remaining wires are at \emph{arbitrary} positions $x_1<x_2<\dots<x_N$; this is what
``asymmetric'' means here.  (In the symmetric case the wires are equally spaced,
$x_i=-\tfrac12+\tfrac{i-1}{N-1}$, with spacing $d=\tfrac1{N-1}$.)  Equation~\eqref{eq:norm}
only fixes the units in which $b$ is measured; the derivation below never uses it except in
the explicit $N=3$ check at the end, because only differences of positions will matter.

Between wire $k$ and wire $k+1$ there is the loop number $k$.  A magnetic field $B$ points
perpendicular to the plane of the device, and we define the normalized field
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bdef}
b=\frac{B\,L\,W}{\Phi_0},\qquad \Phi_0=\frac{h}{2e}.
\end{equation}
The flux through loop $k$ (area $L\cdot W(x_{k+1}-x_k)$) is then $\Phi_0\,b\,(x_{k+1}-x_k)$.
Loop $k$ also carries an integer number $n_k$ of vortices (winding number of the phase around
that loop).

\section{The Meissner phase equation}\label{sec:meissner}
% ===========================================================================
Going once around loop $k$ (between wire $k$ and wire $k+1$), the phase of the order parameter must
come back to itself up to a whole number of turns (fluxoid quantization).  The flux through the loop
contributes the phase $2\pi b\,(x_{k+1}-x_k)$, the two wires contribute $-\varphi_{k+1}+\varphi_k$, and the total
is $2\pi$ times the vortex number $n_k$:
\[
2\pi b\,(x_{k+1}-x_k)-\varphi_{k+1}+\varphi_k=2\pi n_k ,
\]
that is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meissner-step}
\varphi_{k+1}-\varphi_k=2\pi b\,(x_{k+1}-x_k)-2\pi n_k ,\qquad k=1,\dots,N-1 .
\end{equation}
We want every phase in terms of the phase of the first wire.  Write~\eqref{eq:meissner-step}
for $k=1,2,\dots,i-1$ and add the equations.  The phases and the positions telescope,
\[
(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)+\dots+(\varphi_i-\varphi_{i-1})=\varphi_i-\varphi_1 ,\qquad
(x_2-x_1)+\dots+(x_i-x_{i-1})=x_i-x_1 ,
\]
and the vortex numbers simply add up.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meissner}
\boxed{\;\varphi_i=\varphi_1+2\pi b\,(x_i-x_1)-2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k\;}\qquad[\,i=1,2,3,\dots,N\,]
\end{equation}
This is the Meissner phase equation (for $i=1$ it is trivially true).  In the shorthand of the notes,
\[
\varphi_i=\varphi_1+2\pi b\,\Delta x_i-2\pi n_{1,i},\qquad
\Delta x_i=x_i-x_1,\quad n_{1,i}=\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k .
\]

\begin{notebox}{What is different from the symmetric case}
Only the position term changes: $x_i-x_1$ was $\frac{i-1}{N-1}$ for equally spaced wires and is now an arbitrary
number.  The vortex sum $\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k$ is a sum over loops already in the symmetric case and does not change.
\end{notebox}

% ===========================================================================
\section{The characteristic phase}\label{sec:char}
% ===========================================================================
Now we will do the characteristic phase.

\subsection{Definition}
The characteristic phase is the average of the $N$ wire phases:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:char-def}
\varphi=\frac{\varphi_1+\varphi_2+\varphi_3+\cdots+\varphi_N}{N}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\varphi=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi_i .
\end{equation}
It is the one free phase variable of the problem: $N$ wire phases minus the $N-1$ loop
constraints~\eqref{eq:meissner-step} leaves one.  We separate the first wire from the rest,
\[
\varphi=\frac1N\Bigl(\varphi_1+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\varphi_i\Bigr),
\]
and put in the Meissner phase equation~\eqref{eq:meissner} for every wire $i=2,\dots,N$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:char-insert}
\varphi=\frac1N\Biggl(\varphi_1+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\Bigl[\varphi_1+2\pi b\,(x_i-x_1)-2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k\Bigr]\Biggr).
\end{equation}
The sum over $i$ of a bracket with three kinds of terms is the sum of three sums:
\[
\sum_{i=2}^{N}\Bigl[\ \cdot\ \Bigr]=
\underbrace{\sum_{i=2}^{N}\varphi_1}_{\text{(A)}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{i=2}^{N}2\pi b\,(x_i-x_1)}_{\text{(B)}}
-\underbrace{2\pi\sum_{i=2}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k}_{\text{(C)}} .
\]

\subsection{Sum (A)}
$\varphi_1$ does not depend on $i$, so we add the same number once for each of
$i=2,3,\dots,N$.  That is $N-1$ copies:
\[
\sum_{i=2}^{N}\varphi_1=(N-1)\,\varphi_1 .
\]
(If the sum were indexed from $i=1$ it would be $N\varphi_1$.)

\subsection{Sum (B)}
Pull out the constant $2\pi b$ and split the difference:
\[
\sum_{i=2}^{N}2\pi b\,(x_i-x_1)=2\pi b\sum_{i=2}^{N}(x_i-x_1)
=2\pi b\Bigl(\sum_{i=2}^{N}x_i-\sum_{i=2}^{N}x_1\Bigr).
\]
Rewrite the first sum so that it starts at $i=1$ (add and subtract the term $x_1$):
$\sum_{i=2}^{N}x_i=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-x_1$.  The second sum is $N-1$ copies of $x_1$,
that is $\sum_{i=2}^{N}x_1=(N-1)x_1=Nx_1-x_1$.  Hence
\[
2\pi b\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-x_1-Nx_1+x_1\Bigr)=2\pi b\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-Nx_1\Bigr)
=2\pi b\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-2\pi b\,N x_1 .
\]

\subsection{Sum (C): the swap of sums}
We need
\[
\sum_{i=2}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k .
\]
It is a sum over all pairs $(i,k)$ with $2\le i\le N$ and $1\le k\le i-1$, that is, over the
points of a triangle in the $(i,k)$ grid.  The sum as written reads the triangle row by row
(fix $i$ first, then run over $k$).  We can read the same points column by column instead
(fix $k$ first, then run over $i$): the value $k$ occurs for $k=1,\dots,N-1$, and for a given
$k$ the wires $i$ that contain it are $i=k+1,\dots,N$.

\emph{Example, $N=4$} (written out in full):
\[
\sum_{i=2}^{4}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k=n_1+(n_1+n_2)+(n_1+n_2+n_3)=3n_1+2n_2+1n_3 ,
\]
which is $(N-1)n_1+(N-2)n_2+(N-3)n_3$ for $N=4$: the vortex number $n_1$ is counted once for
each of the wires $i=2,3,4$ (three times), $n_2$ for $i=3,4$ (twice), $n_3$ for $i=4$ (once).

\emph{General $N$.}  Swapping the order of the sums,
\[
\sum_{i=2}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}n_k .
\]
The summand $n_k$ does not depend on $i$, so the inner sum just counts how many integers
$i=k+1,\dots,N$ there are:
\[
\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}n_k=n_k\Bigl(\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}1\Bigr)=n_k\,(N-k).
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swap}
\boxed{\;\sum_{i=2}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)\,n_k\;}
\end{equation}
(The same identity holds with the outer sum starting at $i=1$, because the $i=1$ term is the
empty sum $0$.)  The upper limit is $N-1$: the last loop is loop $N-1$.

\subsection{Putting (A), (B), (C) back}
Now we can add all these back to the original equation~\eqref{eq:char-insert}:
\begin{align}
\varphi&=\frac1N\Bigl(\varphi_1+(N-1)\varphi_1+2\pi b\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-2\pi b\,Nx_1-2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k\Bigr)\notag\\
&=\frac1N\Bigl(N\varphi_1+2\pi b\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-2\pi b\,Nx_1-2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k\Bigr)\notag\\
&=\frac1N\Bigl(N\varphi_1+2\pi b\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-Nx_1\Bigr)\Bigr)-\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k\notag\\
&=\varphi_1+\frac1N\,2\pi b\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-2\pi b\,x_1-\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k\notag\\
&=\varphi_1+2\pi b\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-x_1\Bigr)-\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k .\label{eq:char-result}
\end{align}
Solve for $\varphi_1$ (move the two last terms to the other side):
\[
\varphi_1=\varphi-2\pi b\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-x_1\Bigr)+\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k ,
\]
and reverse the sign inside the first bracket:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi1}
\boxed{\;\varphi_1=\varphi+2\pi b\Bigl(x_1-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\Bigr)+\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)\,n_k\;}
\end{equation}
The phase of the first wire differs from the characteristic phase by a field part (the position
of wire 1 relative to the mean position of all wires) and a vortex part.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Back into the Meissner phase equation: $\varphi_i$ in terms of $\varphi$}\label{sec:phii}
% ===========================================================================
Now we put $\varphi_1$ that we derived back into the Meissner phase equation:
\begin{align*}
\varphi_1&=\varphi+2\pi b\Bigl(x_1-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\Bigr)+\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)n_k ,\\
\varphi_i&=\varphi_1+2\pi b\,(x_i-x_1)-2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}n_k .
\end{align*}

\textbf{Note about dummy indices.}  In the formula for $\varphi_1$ the sum $\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i$
has no free label: it is one single number.  In $\varphi_i$, however, the letter $i$ is the free
wire label, and it cannot also be the dummy letter of a sum inside the same formula.  So we
rename the dummies (this changes nothing, e.g.\ $\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i=\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k$ is the same
number): the position sum uses $k$, the weighted vortex sum $\sum(N-k)n_k$ uses $p$, and the
partial vortex sum uses $j$.  Substituting,
\begin{align*}
\varphi_i&=\varphi+2\pi b\Bigl(x_1-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)+\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p
 +2\pi b\,(x_i-x_1)-2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\\[2pt]
\varphi_i&=\varphi+2\pi b\,x_1-2\pi b\,\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k+\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p
 +2\pi b\,x_i-2\pi b\,x_1-2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\\[2pt]
\varphi_i&=\varphi-2\pi b\,\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k+\frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p
 +2\pi b\,x_i-2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j ,
\end{align*}
where in the last line $+2\pi b\,x_1-2\pi b\,x_1=0$ cancelled.  Now we group the terms that carry the
field $b$ and the terms that carry the vortex numbers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-i}
\boxed{\;\varphi_i=\varphi+2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)
+2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)\,n_p-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr)\;}
\end{equation}
The first bracket is the position of wire $i$ relative to the mean position of all the wires; the
second bracket is the average vortex term minus the vortices to the left of wire $i$.  The part of
$\varphi_i$ that is not $\varphi$ we call $z_i=\varphi_i-\varphi$.

\begin{paritybox}{1: the formula for $\varphi_i$ at $N=3$}
Everything that follows is built on~\eqref{eq:phi-i}, so we test it once on the smallest
asymmetric case, $N=3$ with $x_1=-\tfrac12,\ x_2$ free, $x_3=+\tfrac12$.  Call the two vortex
numbers $n_{12}\equiv n_1$ and $n_{23}\equiv n_2$.  For $N=3$:
$\sum_{k}x_k=x_2$ and $\sum_p(N-p)n_p=2n_{12}+n_{23}$.  Then~\eqref{eq:phi-i} gives
\begin{align*}
z_1&=2\pi\Bigl[b\bigl(-\tfrac12-\tfrac13x_2\bigr)+\tfrac13(2n_{12}+n_{23})\Bigr],\\
z_2&=2\pi\Bigl[b\bigl(x_2-\tfrac13x_2\bigr)+\tfrac13(2n_{12}+n_{23})-n_{12}\Bigr]
   =2\pi\Bigl[\tfrac23\,b\,x_2+\tfrac13(n_{23}-n_{12})\Bigr],\\
z_3&=2\pi\Bigl[b\bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac13x_2\bigr)+\tfrac13(2n_{12}+n_{23})-n_{12}-n_{23}\Bigr]
   =2\pi\Bigl[b\bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac13x_2\bigr)-\tfrac13(n_{12}+2n_{23})\Bigr].
\end{align*}
\emph{(i) The mean of the three phases must be $\varphi$, i.e.\ $z_1+z_2+z_3=0$.}
The $b$-terms give $-\tfrac12-\tfrac13x_2+\tfrac23x_2+\tfrac12-\tfrac13x_2=0$ and the vortex terms give
$\tfrac13\bigl[(2n_{12}+n_{23})+(n_{23}-n_{12})-(n_{12}+2n_{23})\bigr]=0$.  \checkmark

\emph{(ii) The differences must reproduce the Meissner relation~\eqref{eq:meissner-step}.}
\begin{align*}
\varphi_2-\varphi_1=z_2-z_1&=2\pi\Bigl[b\bigl(x_2+\tfrac12\bigr)-n_{12}\Bigr]=2\pi b\,(x_2-x_1)-2\pi n_{12},\\
\varphi_3-\varphi_2=z_3-z_2&=2\pi\Bigl[b\bigl(\tfrac12-x_2\bigr)-n_{23}\Bigr]=2\pi b\,(x_3-x_2)-2\pi n_{23}.\qquad\checkmark
\end{align*}
\end{paritybox}

% ===========================================================================
\section{The Likharev CPR and the cube}
% ===========================================================================
Now that we have the equation we will plug this into the Likharev CPR.

\subsection{The CPR of $N$ wires in parallel}
For every wire the (cubic) Likharev current--phase relation is
\[
I_i=I_0\Bigl[\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}-\Bigl(\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}\Bigr)^{3}\Bigr],
\]
where $I_0$ and $\varphi_0$ are the current and phase scales of the wire; we keep $\varphi_0$
as a symbol throughout.  The wires are in parallel, so the total current is
$I=\sum_{i=1}^{N}I_i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likharev}
\frac{I}{I_0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl[\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}-\Bigl(\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}\Bigr)^{3}\Bigr].
\end{equation}
Inserting the boxed formula~\eqref{eq:phi-i} for $\varphi_i$,
\begin{align*}
\frac{I}{I_0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Biggl(&\frac{1}{\varphi_0}\Bigl[\varphi+2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)+2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\Vs-\Ps\Bigr)\Bigr]\\
&-\frac{1}{\varphi_0^{3}}\Bigl[\varphi+2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)+2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\Vs-\Ps\Bigr)\Bigr]^{3}\Biggr).
\end{align*}
(The factors $1/\varphi_0$ and $1/\varphi_0^{3}$ have been written in front of the brackets.)  The first part is the \emph{linear} part, the second the \emph{cubic} part.  We will look at the
cubic term first.

\subsection{Naming the pieces}
The thing that is cubed is $\varphi$ plus two more pieces.  We will let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ac-def}
a_i=2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c_i=2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)\,n_p-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr),
\end{equation}
because naming the second piece $b$ would confuse it with the magnetic field.  Then
$\varphi_i=\varphi+a_i+c_i$ (so $z_i=a_i+c_i$): $a_i$ is the \emph{position} (field) part and $c_i$ the
\emph{vortex} part.  The dependence on $i$ is shown by the subscript.

\subsection{Expanding the cube}
We expand $(\varphi+a_i+c_i)^3$ by treating $a_i+c_i$ as one object:
\[
(\varphi+a_i+c_i)^{3}=\varphi^{3}+3\varphi^{2}(a_i+c_i)+3\varphi\,(a_i+c_i)^{2}+(a_i+c_i)^{3}.
\]
With $(a_i+c_i)^2=a_i^2+2a_ic_i+c_i^2$ and $(a_i+c_i)^3=a_i^3+3a_i^2c_i+3a_ic_i^2+c_i^3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ten-terms}
(\varphi+a_i+c_i)^{3}=\varphi^{3}+a_i^{3}+c_i^{3}+3\varphi^{2}a_i+3\varphi^{2}c_i+3a_i^{2}\varphi
+3a_i^{2}c_i+3c_i^{2}\varphi+3c_i^{2}a_i+6\varphi a_ic_i .
\end{equation}
There are ten terms.  (A count as a sanity check: putting $\varphi=a_i=c_i=1$ the left side is $3^3=27$
and the right side is $1+1+1+3\cdot6+6=27$.)  We number them as in the notes and place the
summation over the collected terms:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\underset{1}{\varphi^{3}}+\underset{2}{a_i^{3}}+\underset{3}{c_i^{3}}
+\underset{4}{3\varphi^{2}a_i}+\underset{5}{3\varphi^{2}c_i}&+\underset{6}{3a_i^{2}\varphi}\\
&+\underset{7}{3a_i^{2}c_i}+\underset{8}{3c_i^{2}\varphi}+\underset{9}{3c_i^{2}a_i}+\underset{10}{6\varphi a_ic_i}\Bigr).
\end{align*}
Each of the ten terms is summed over $i=1,\dots,N$ separately in Section~\ref{sec:ten}.

\section{Summation rules used repeatedly}\label{sec:rules}
% ===========================================================================
Before doing the ten sums we collect the rules that they use.  None of them is new -- the
first two were used in Section~\ref{sec:char} -- but they are written once, with their reasons, so that each
of the ten sums can simply refer to them.

\begin{notebox}{The rules online}
These are the standard rules for working with sums.  The pages below explain them with worked examples.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]

\item \textbf{Rules 1 and 2} (swapping the order of a triangular double sum, with and without a weight):

 \url{https://proofwiki.org/wiki/Summation_of_i_from_1_to_n_of_Summation_of_j_from_1_to_i/Example}\\

\textbf{Rule 0 (constants and renaming).}
(a) If $K$ does not depend on $i$, then $\sum_{i=1}^{N}K=N\,K$, and $K$ can be pulled out of
$\sum_{i}$.  (b) \emph{Independent of $i$:} $\varphi$, $b$, $N$, $\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k$ and
$\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p$.  \emph{Depending on $i$:} $x_i$ and the partial sum $\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j$.
(c) A dummy index can be renamed: $\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i=\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k$.

\textbf{Rule 1 (swap of sums).}
For numbers $f_1,\dots,f_{N-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule1}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}f_j=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)\,f_j .
\end{equation}
This is~\eqref{eq:swap} (the $i=1$ term is empty).  Reason: the pairs $(i,j)$ with $1\le j\le i-1$
are read column by column; for fixed $j$ the wires are $i=j+1,\dots,N$, which is $N-j$ of them.

\textbf{Rule 2 (swap of sums with a weight).}
If the summand carries a factor $g_i$ that depends on $i$, the number of wires is replaced by the
sum of the weights:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule2}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_i\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}g_i .
\end{equation}
The pair $(i,j)$ with $j\le i-1$ contributes $g_i n_j$; for fixed $j$ the allowed $i$ are
$j+1,\dots,N$, so $n_j$ multiplies $\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}g_i$.  For $g_i=1$ this is Rule~1, because
$\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}1=N-j$.  Taking $g_i=x_i$ or $g_i=x_i^{2}$ produces the \emph{tail sums}
$\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i$ and $\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i^{2}$ of the positions of the wires to the right of
loop $j$; these are the places where the asymmetry of the wire positions enters.

\textbf{Rule 3 (square of the partial sum).}
The square of a sum is a double sum,
$\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\bigr)^{2}=\sum_{j,l\le i-1}n_jn_l$.  The diagonal terms $j=l$ give $n_j^2$,
and every off-diagonal pair $j\ne l$ appears twice, as $(j,l)$ and $(l,j)$, with the same value:
\[
\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{i-2}\sum_{l=j+1}^{i-1}n_jn_l .
\]
Multiply by $g_i$ and sum over $i$.  For the diagonal part use Rule~2 with $n_j\to n_j^{2}$.  For the
pair $(j,l)$ with $j<l$: it is present in wire $i$ exactly when $l\le i-1$, i.e.\ $i\ge l+1$, so
it is multiplied by $\sum_{i=l+1}^{N}g_i$; the pairs have $j=1,\dots,N-2$ and $l=j+1,\dots,N-1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule3}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_i\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j^{2}\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}g_i
+2\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}\sum_{l=j+1}^{N-1}n_jn_l\sum_{i=l+1}^{N}g_i .
\end{equation}
For $g_i=1$ (and $\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}1=N-j$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule3b}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr)^{2}=\Sq .
\end{equation}
\emph{Example, $N=3$:} the left side is $0+n_1^{2}+(n_1+n_2)^{2}=2n_1^{2}+2n_1n_2+n_2^{2}$ and the right
side is $(3-1)n_1^{2}+(3-2)n_2^{2}+2(3-2)n_1n_2$, the same.

\textbf{Rule 4 (cube of the partial sum).}
Cubing a sum gives a triple sum $\sum_{j,l,q\le i-1}n_jn_ln_q$.  Sorting the triples: all three indices
equal gives $n_j^{3}$; exactly two equal gives $n_j^{2}n_l$ with $j\neq l$, and there are $3$ choices of which
slot holds the odd index, so $3\sum_{j\ne l}n_j^{2}n_l$ over ordered pairs; all three different gives
$n_jn_ln_q$ for each unordered triple $j<l<q$, which occurs in $3!=6$ orders.  A monomial whose largest
index is $r$ is present in wire $i$ exactly when $r\le i-1$, which is true for $N-r$ wires.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule4}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr)^{3}=\Cu .
\end{equation}
\emph{Example, $N=3$:} the left side is $n_1^{3}+(n_1+n_2)^{3}=2n_1^{3}+3n_1^{2}n_2+3n_1n_2^{2}+n_2^{3}$ and the right side
is $2n_1^{3}+n_2^{3}+3\bigl[(3-2)n_1^{2}n_2+(3-2)n_2^{2}n_1\bigr]$ (no triples when $N=3$), the same.

\section{The ten sums}\label{sec:ten}
% ===========================================================================
Now we sum each of the ten terms of~\eqref{eq:ten-terms} over $i=1,\dots,N$, one by one and raw, term by
term.  The rules of Section~\ref{sec:rules} are quoted by number.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 1: $\sum_i\varphi^{3}$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
$\varphi$ is the same for every wire, so we add the same number $N$ times:
\[
\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi^{3}=N\varphi^{3}\;}
\]

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 2: $\sum_i a_i^{3}$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Start from $a_i=2\pi b\bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k}x_k\bigr)=2\pi b\,x_i-\frac{2\pi b}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k$ and cube
the difference with $(P-Q)^3=P^3-3P^2Q+3PQ^2-Q^3$, here with $P=2\pi b\,x_i$ and
$Q=\frac{2\pi b}{N}\sum_k x_k$ (written out, no new letters):
\begin{align*}
a_i^{3}&=\Bigl(2\pi b\,x_i-\frac{2\pi b}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}\\
&=(2\pi b\,x_i)^{3}-3(2\pi b\,x_i)^{2}\Bigl(\frac{2\pi b}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)
+3(2\pi b\,x_i)\Bigl(\frac{2\pi b}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{2\pi b}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}\\
&=8\pi^{3}b^{3}x_i^{3}-\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}x_i^{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k
+\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}x_i}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}-\frac{8\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}.
\end{align*}
(For instance $3(2\pi b\,x_i)^2\cdot\frac{2\pi b}{N}=3\cdot4\pi^2b^2x_i^2\cdot\frac{2\pi b}{N}=\frac{24\pi^3b^3x_i^2}{N}$.)
Now we sum each of the four pieces over $i=1,\dots,N$.  The sum $\sum_{k}x_k$ does not depend on $i$
(Rule~0), so it is a constant:
\begin{align*}
&\sum_{i=1}^{N}8\pi^{3}b^{3}x_i^{3}=8\pi^{3}b^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{3},\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}x_i^{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k=-\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2},\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}x_i}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}=\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i=\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3},\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\frac{8\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}=-N\cdot\frac{8\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}=-\frac{8\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}.
\end{align*}
In the third line $\sum_i x_i=\sum_k x_k$ (Rule~0c).  The last two combine: $\frac{24-8}{N^{2}}=\frac{16}{N^{2}}$.  So
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i^{3}&=8\pi^{3}b^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{3}-\frac{24\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}
+\frac{16\pi^{3}b^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}
\end{align*}
and pulling out $8\pi^3b^3$:
\begin{resbox}
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i^{3}=8\pi^{3}b^{3}\Biggl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{3}-\frac{3}{N}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}\Bigr)+\frac{2}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{3}\Biggr]
\]
\end{resbox}

\begin{paritybox}{2: the cubic position term, symmetry and $N=3$}
Term 2 is the only place where the cube of the position part enters and it carries all of the
asymmetry in the $b^3$ term, so we test it.

\emph{(i) Parity under reflection.}  Put the wires symmetrically about the centre, $x_{N+1-i}=-x_i$.  Then
$\sum_{k}x_k=0$ and $\sum_i x_i^{3}=0$ (odd powers cancel in pairs), so $\sum_i a_i^{3}=0$.
This is as it must be: here $a_{N+1-i}=-a_i$, so the cubes cancel in pairs.  The formula therefore gives a
$b^{3}$ term in $R$ only when the wire positions are asymmetric.

\emph{(ii) $N=3$, $x=(-\tfrac12,\,x_2,\,+\tfrac12)$.}  Here $\sum_k x_k=x_2$,
$\sum_i x_i^{2}=\tfrac12+x_2^{2}$, $\sum_i x_i^{3}=x_2^{3}$ (the cubes of $\mp\tfrac12$ cancel).  The result gives
\[
8\pi^{3}b^{3}\Bigl[x_2^{3}-\tfrac{3}{3}\,x_2\bigl(\tfrac12+x_2^{2}\bigr)+\tfrac29x_2^{3}\Bigr]
=8\pi^{3}b^{3}\Bigl[-\tfrac12x_2+\tfrac29x_2^{3}\Bigr]=-4\pi^{3}b^{3}x_2+\tfrac{16}{9}\pi^{3}b^{3}x_2^{3}.
\]
Directly, with $u=x_2/3$: $a_1=-2\pi b(\tfrac12+u)$, $a_2=2\pi b\,(2u)$, $a_3=2\pi b(\tfrac12-u)$, and
\[
\frac{\sum_i a_i^{3}}{8\pi^{3}b^{3}}=\bigl(\tfrac12-u\bigr)^{3}-\bigl(\tfrac12+u\bigr)^{3}+8u^{3}
=-\tfrac32u-2u^{3}+8u^{3}=-\tfrac32u+6u^{3}=-\tfrac12x_2+\tfrac29x_2^{3},
\]
the same.  \checkmark
\end{paritybox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 3: $\sum_i c_i^{3}$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Here $c_i=2\pi\bigl(\frac1N\sum_{p}(N-p)n_p-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\bigr)$, so $c_i^{3}=(2\pi)^{3}(\,\cdot\,)^{3}$ and
we cube the difference again, $(P-Q)^3=P^3-3P^2Q+3PQ^2-Q^3$, with
$P=\frac1N\sum_p(N-p)n_p$ and $Q=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j$:
\begin{align*}
c_i^{3}=8\pi^{3}\Biggl[&\frac{1}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}-\frac{3}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)\\
&+\frac{3}{N}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}-\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{3}\Biggr].
\end{align*}
We sum the four pieces over $i=1,\dots,N$ one at a time (``pieces of $c^3$'', to keep them apart from the ten
terms).

\textbf{Piece 1.}  Nothing depends on $i$:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{8\pi^{3}}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}=N\cdot\frac{8\pi^{3}}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}=\frac{8\pi^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}.
\]
\textbf{Piece 2.}  The factor $(\sum_p(N-p)n_p)^2$ comes out; the swap of sums (Rule~1) gives
$\sum_i\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j$, which is the same number as $\sum_p(N-p)n_p$:
\begin{align*}
-\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j
&=-\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\\
&=-\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}.
\end{align*}
\textbf{Piece 3.}  Here we need the sum of the square of the partial sum, Rule~3 with $g_i=1$,
equation~\eqref{eq:rule3b}:
\begin{align*}
\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}
&=\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl[\SqA\\
&\qquad+\SqB\Bigr].
\end{align*}
\textbf{Piece 4.}  The cube of the partial sum, Rule~4, equation~\eqref{eq:rule4}:
\begin{align*}
-8\pi^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{3}&=-8\pi^{3}\Bigl[\CuA+\CuB\\
&\qquad+\CuC\Bigr].
\end{align*}
Adding the four pieces, exactly as they come out (this is the form in the notes, now with the sums over $i$ carried out):
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i^{3}=\ &\frac{8\pi^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}-\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{3}\\
&+\frac{24\pi^{3}}{N}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl[\SqA+\SqB\Bigr]\\
&-8\pi^{3}\Bigl[\CuA+\CuB+\CuC\Bigr].
\end{align*}
Pieces 1 and 2 have the same structure, $\frac{8-24}{N^2}=-\frac{16}{N^2}=8\cdot\bigl(-\frac{2}{N^{2}}\bigr)$:
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i^{3}&=\ResThree
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 4: $\sum_i 3\varphi^{2}a_i$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\[
3\varphi^{2}a_i=3\varphi^{2}\Bigl(2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigr)=6\pi b\varphi^{2}x_i-6\pi b\varphi^{2}\,\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k .
\]
Sum over $i$.  The first piece is $6\pi b\varphi^{2}\sum_{i}x_i$.  In the second piece the number
$\sum_{k}x_k$ does not depend on $i$, so adding it $N$ times cancels the $\frac1N$:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3\varphi^{2}a_i=6\pi b\varphi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-\frac{6\pi b\varphi^{2}}{N}\,N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k
=6\pi b\varphi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-6\pi b\varphi^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k=0 .
\]
\[
\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}3\varphi^{2}a_i=0\;}
\]

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 5: $\sum_i 3\varphi^{2}c_i$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\[
3\varphi^{2}c_i=3\varphi^{2}\Bigl(2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\Vs-\Ps\Bigr)\Bigr)=\frac{6\pi\varphi^{2}}{N}\Vs-6\pi\varphi^{2}\Ps .
\]
Sum over $i$: the first piece is the same for all $i$, giving a factor $N$ that cancels the $\frac1N$; the
second is the swap of sums (Rule~1):
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3\varphi^{2}c_i=6\pi\varphi^{2}\Vs-6\pi\varphi^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j=0 ,
\]
because the two sums are the same sum with different dummy letters.
\[
\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}3\varphi^{2}c_i=0\;}
\]

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 6: $\sum_i 3a_i^{2}\varphi$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Square the difference, $(x_i-m)^2=x_i^2-2x_im+m^2$ with $m=\frac1N\sum_kx_k$ (written out):
\[
3a_i^{2}\varphi=3\Bigl(2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2}\varphi
=3\cdot4\pi^{2}b^{2}\Bigl(x_i^{2}-2x_i\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k+\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\varphi .
\]
So $\sum_i3a_i^{2}\varphi=12\pi^{2}b^{2}\varphi\bigl(\text{(6.1)}+\text{(6.2)}+\text{(6.3)}\bigr)$ with the three pieces
\begin{align*}
\text{(6.1)}&\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}\qquad\text{(stays the same)},\\
\text{(6.2)}&\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{N}2x_i\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k=-\frac2N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i=-\frac2N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2},\\
\text{(6.3)}&\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}=N\cdot\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}=\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}.
\end{align*}
Adding, $-\frac2N+\frac1N=-\frac1N$:
\begin{resbox}
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3a_i^{2}\varphi=12\pi^{2}b^{2}\varphi\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]
\]
\end{resbox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 7: $\sum_i 3a_i^{2}c_i$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Using the square of $a_i$ from Term~6 and the definition of $c_i$,
\[
3a_i^{2}c_i=3\Bigl(4\pi^{2}b^{2}\Bigl(x_i^{2}-2x_i\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k+\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigl(2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\Vs-\Ps\Bigr)\Bigr),
\]
and the numerical prefactor is $3\cdot4\pi^{2}b^{2}\cdot2\pi=24\pi^{3}b^{2}$:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3a_i^{2}c_i=24\pi^{3}b^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl[x_i^{2}-\frac2Nx_i\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k+\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\Bigl[\frac1N\Vs-\Ps\Bigr].
\]
Multiplying the $3\times2=6$ terms:
\begin{align*}
&\text{(7.1)}\;\; x_i^{2}\cdot\frac1N\Vs, &&\text{(7.2)}\;\; -x_i^{2}\Ps,\\
&\text{(7.3)}\;\; -\frac2Nx_i\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\cdot\frac1N\Vs, &&\text{(7.4)}\;\; +\frac2Nx_i\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Ps,\\
&\text{(7.5)}\;\; \frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\cdot\frac1N\Vs, &&\text{(7.6)}\;\; -\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Ps .
\end{align*}
We sum each over $i=1,\dots,N$ (the common factor $24\pi^3b^2$ is put back at the end):
\begin{align*}
\text{(7.1)}&\;\;\frac1N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}
&&\text{only }x_i^{2}\text{ depends on }i;\\
\text{(7.2)}&\;\;-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i^{2}
&&\text{Rule 2 with }g_i=x_i^{2};\\
\text{(7.3)}&\;\;-\frac2{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\\
&\;\;=-\frac2{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Vs
&&\sum_ix_i=\sum_kx_k;\\
\text{(7.4)}&\;\;+\frac2N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i
&&\text{Rule 2 with }g_i=x_i;\\
\text{(7.5)}&\;\;N\cdot\frac1{N^{3}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Vs=\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Vs
&&\text{nothing depends on }i;\\
\text{(7.6)}&\;\;-\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j\\
&\;\;=-\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Vs
&&\text{Rule 1}.
\end{align*}
Pieces (7.5) and (7.6) are equal and opposite and cancel.  Collecting (7.1)--(7.4):
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3a_i^{2}c_i&=\ResSeven
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 8: $\sum_i 3c_i^{2}\varphi$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Square the difference, $(P-Q)^2=P^2-2PQ+Q^2$ with $P=\frac1N\sum_p(N-p)n_p$ and $Q=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j$:
\[
3c_i^{2}\varphi=3\varphi\cdot4\pi^{2}\Biggl[\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)+\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr].
\]
Summing over each piece of $i$ (the factor $12\pi^{2}\varphi$ is put back at the end):
\begin{align*}
\text{(8.1)}&\;\;N\cdot\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}=\frac1N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2},\\
\text{(8.2)}&\;\;-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j=-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j\\
&\;\;=-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}
\qquad\text{(the constant comes out; then Rule 1)},\\
\text{(8.3)}&\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}=\SqA\\
&\qquad\qquad+\SqB\qquad\text{(Rule 3)}.
\end{align*}
Adding, $\frac1N-\frac2N=-\frac1N$:
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3c_i^{2}\varphi&=\ResEight
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 9: $\sum_i 3c_i^{2}a_i$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Using $c_i^{2}$ from Term~8 and $a_i=2\pi b\bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_kx_k\bigr)$, the prefactor is
$3\cdot4\pi^{2}\cdot2\pi b=24\pi^{3}b$:
\[
3c_i^{2}a_i=24\pi^{3}b\,\Biggl[\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)+\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr]
\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr).
\]
Multiplying out the $3\times2=6$ terms:
\begin{align*}
&\text{(9.1)}\;\;\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}x_i, &&\text{(9.2)}\;\;-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)x_i\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr),\\
&\text{(9.3)}\;\;x_i\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}, &&\text{(9.4)}\;\;-\frac1{N^{3}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2},\\
&\text{(9.5)}\;\;+\frac2{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr), &&\text{(9.6)}\;\;-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Ps\Bigr)^{2}.
\end{align*}
We have to sum each over $i=1,\dots,N$:
\begin{align*}
\text{(9.1)}&\;\;\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i=\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr),\\
\text{(9.2)}&\;\;-\frac2N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i\qquad\text{(Rule 2 with }g_i=x_i),\\
\text{(9.3)}&\;\;\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j^{2}\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i+2\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}\sum_{l=j+1}^{N-1}n_jn_l\sum_{i=l+1}^{N}x_i\qquad\text{(Rule 3 with }g_i=x_i),\\
\text{(9.4)}&\;\;-N\cdot\frac1{N^{3}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}=-\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2},\\
\text{(9.5)}&\;\;\frac2{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j=\frac2{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\qquad\text{(Rule 1)},\\
\text{(9.6)}&\;\;-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl[\SqA+\SqB\Bigr]\qquad\text{(Rule 3 with }g_i=1).
\end{align*}
The three pieces that contain $\bigl(\sum_k x_k\bigr)\bigl(\sum_p(N-p)n_p\bigr)^{2}$ combine:
$\frac1{N^{2}}-\frac1{N^{2}}+\frac2{N^{2}}=\frac2{N^{2}}$ (pieces (9.1), (9.4), (9.5)).  Therefore
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}3c_i^{2}a_i&=\ResNine
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Term 10: $\sum_i 6\varphi a_ic_i$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\[
a_i=2\pi b\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr),\qquad c_i=2\pi\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr).
\]
Multiplying it out, with the prefactor $6\varphi\cdot2\pi b\cdot2\pi=24\pi^{2}b\varphi$:
\begin{align*}
6\varphi a_ic_i&=24\pi^{2}b\varphi\Bigl(x_i-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr)\\
&=24\pi^{2}b\varphi\Bigl(x_i\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p-x_i\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p+\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr).
\end{align*}
We sum the four terms over $i$:
\begin{align*}
\text{(10.1)}&\;\;\frac1N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\qquad\text{(only }x_i\text{ depends on }i),\\
\text{(10.2)}&\;\;-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i\qquad\text{(Rule 2 with }g_i=x_i),\\
\text{(10.3)}&\;\;-N\cdot\frac1{N^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Vs=-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Vs,\\
\text{(10.4)}&\;\;\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j=\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)\Vs\qquad\text{(Rule 1)}.
\end{align*}
Terms (10.3) and (10.4) are equal and opposite, so they cancel.  What is left is
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}6\varphi a_ic_i&=\ResTen
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}

% ===========================================================================
\section{All ten sums together}
% ===========================================================================
We have all ten summations.  Adding them gives the sum of the cube over all wires:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\varphi+a_i+c_i\bigr)^{3}=\ &N\varphi^{3}\ \ \text{[term 1]}\\
&+\ResTwo\ \ \text{[term 2]}\\
&+\ResThree\ \ \text{[term 3]}\\
&+0\qquad\text{[terms 4 and 5]}\\
&+\ResSix\ \ \text{[term 6]}\\
&+\ResSeven\ \ \text{[term 7]}\\
&+\ResEight\ \ \text{[term 8]}\\
&+\ResNine\ \ \text{[term 9]}\\
&+\ResTen\ \ \text{[term 10]}
\end{align*}
Sorted by the power of the characteristic phase: term 1 is $\propto\varphi^{3}$; terms 6, 8, 10 are $\propto\varphi$;
terms 2, 3, 7, 9 do not contain $\varphi$; the $\varphi^{2}$ terms 4 and 5 vanish.

% ===========================================================================
\section{The linear part}
% ===========================================================================
We solved the cubic part; let us look at the linear part.  From~\eqref{eq:likharev},
\[
\frac{I}{I_0}=\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}}_{\text{linear}}-\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}\Bigr)^{3}}_{\text{cubic}} ,
\qquad
\text{linear:}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\varphi+a_i+c_i}{\varphi_0}=\frac1{\varphi_0}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi+\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i+\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i\Bigr].
\]
We take the three sums in turn:
\begin{align*}
\text{(1)}&\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi=N\varphi,\\
\text{(2)}&\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i=2\pi b\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-N\cdot\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr]=2\pi b\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i-\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr]=0,\\
\text{(3)}&\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i=2\pi\Bigl[N\cdot\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_j\Bigr]
=2\pi\Bigl[\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j\Bigr]=0 .
\end{align*}
In (3) the swap of sums (Rule~1) was used.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\varphi_i}{\varphi_0}=\frac{N\varphi}{\varphi_0}\;}
\]
(The vanishing of $\sum_ia_i$ and $\sum_ic_i$ is the same fact as the vanishing of Terms~4 and~5: the
deviations from the mean add up to zero.)

% ===========================================================================
\section{Assembling $A$, $D$ and $R$}
% ===========================================================================
Combining the linear part and the cubic part,
\[
\frac{I}{I_0}=\frac{N\varphi}{\varphi_0}-\frac1{\varphi_0^{3}}\Bigl[\text{all the ten terms we calculated}\Bigr].
\]
We need to sort by coefficients of $\varphi$ for $A$, $D$ and $R$, in the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ADR-form}
\frac{I}{I_0}=A\varphi-D\varphi^{3}-R .
\end{equation}

\textbf{$D$: the $\varphi^{3}$ term.}  Only Term~1 contains $\varphi^{3}$:
$-\frac{1}{\varphi_0^{3}}N\varphi^{3}$, hence
\begin{equation}
D=\frac{N}{\varphi_0^{3}} .
\end{equation}

\textbf{$A$: the terms linear in $\varphi$.}  Terms 6, 8 and 10 have a factor $\varphi$:
\begin{align*}
\text{6:}&\quad12\pi^{2}b^{2}\varphi\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\\
\text{8:}&\quad12\pi^{2}\varphi\Bigl[\SqA+\SqB-\frac1N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\\
\text{10:}&\quad24\pi^{2}b\varphi\Bigl[\frac1N\Bigl(\Vs\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i\Bigr]
\end{align*}
and the linear part gives $\frac{N\varphi}{\varphi_0}=\frac{N\varphi_0^{2}}{\varphi_0^{3}}\varphi$.  Writing
$A\varphi=\frac{N\varphi}{\varphi_0}-\frac1{\varphi_0^{3}}(6+8+10)$ and dividing by $\varphi$:
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
A=\frac1{\varphi_0^{3}}\Biggl\{&N\varphi_0^{2}-12\pi^{2}b^{2}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i^{2}-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\\
&-24\pi^{2}b\Bigl[\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k\Bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}n_j\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}x_i\Bigr]\\
&-12\pi^{2}\Bigl[\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)n_j^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}\sum_{l=j+1}^{N-1}(N-l)n_jn_l\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac1N\Bigl(\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(N-p)n_p\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\Biggr\}
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}

\textbf{$R$: the terms without $\varphi$.}  Now the remaining sums belong to $R$: terms 2, 7, 9, 3.  Since the
cubic part enters with a minus sign in~\eqref{eq:ADR-form} and $R$ is subtracted, $R$ is
$\frac1{\varphi_0^{3}}$ times their sum:
\begin{resbox}
\begin{align*}
R=\frac1{\varphi_0^{3}}\Biggl\{&\ \ResTwo\\
&+\ResSeven\\
&+\ResNine\\
&+\ResThree\ \Biggr\}
\end{align*}
\end{resbox}
In the last line the four pieces of Term~3 have already been added up as in the box of Term~3, with the sums
over $i$ carried out.

So, collecting,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CPR-final}
\boxed{\;\frac{I}{I_0}=A\varphi-D\varphi^{3}-R,\qquad D=\frac{N}{\varphi_0^{3}}\;}
\end{equation}
with $A$ and $R$ the explicit expressions above.

% ===========================================================================
\section{The Hamiltonian}\label{sec:ham}
% ===========================================================================
The wire phases are $\varphi_i=\varphi+z_i$, where the shifts $z_i=a_i+c_i$ are fixed numbers
(they depend on the wire positions, on $b$ and on the vortex numbers $n_k$, but not on $\varphi$).  So when the
characteristic phase changes by $d\varphi$, every wire phase changes by the same $d\varphi$, and the energy
$U$ stored in the junctions obeys, wire by wire, $\partial U_i/\partial\varphi_i=\frac{\hbar}{2e}I_i$.  Adding the
$N$ wires,
\[
\frac{dU}{d\varphi}=\frac{\hbar}{2e}\sum_{i=1}^{N}I_i=\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}\,\frac{I}{I_0}
=\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}\bigl(A\varphi-D\varphi^{3}-R\bigr).
\]
Integrating over $\varphi$ (and dropping the constant),
\[
U(\varphi)=\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}\Bigl(\tfrac12A\varphi^{2}-\tfrac14D\varphi^{4}-R\varphi\Bigr).
\]
For the kinetic term nothing changes compared to the symmetric case: the loop constraints fix the $N-1$
relative phases and leave $\varphi$ as the only dynamical variable.  With the charge $Q=2e\hat n$, where
$\hat n=-i\,\partial_\varphi$ is conjugate to $\varphi$, and $E_C=e^{2}/2C$, the charging energy is
$\frac{Q^{2}}{2C}=4E_C\hat n^{2}=-4E_C\,\partial_\varphi^{2}$.  Hence
\begin{resbox}
\[
\hat H=-4E_C\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}}+\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}\Bigl(\frac12A\varphi^{2}-\frac14D\varphi^{4}-R\varphi\Bigr)
\]
\end{resbox}
\begin{notebox}{On the sign of the potential term}
The sign in front of $\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}$ is $+$: the current is the
derivative of the energy, $I=\frac{2e}{\hbar}\,\partial U/\partial\varphi$.  A check is the ordinary Josephson junction,
$I=I_c\sin\varphi$, $U=-E_J\cos\varphi$ with $E_J=\hbar I_c/2e$, so $\partial U/\partial\varphi=+\frac{\hbar I_c}{2e}\sin\varphi$.
Equivalently, for small $\varphi$ the term $\frac{\hbar I_0}{2e}\cdot\frac12A\varphi^{2}$ must have positive curvature when $A>0$
(the junction sits in a minimum at $\varphi\approx0$); with a minus sign it would sit on a maximum.
\end{notebox}

% ===========================================================================
\section{Parity check of the final result}
% ===========================================================================
\begin{paritybox}{3: $N=2$ and $N=3$ limits of $A$, $D$, $R$}
The general formulas must contain the two earlier results as special cases.

\emph{$N=2$.}  Two wires $x_1=-\tfrac12$, $x_2=+\tfrac12$ and one loop with vortex number $n_1$.  Then
$\sum_kx_k=0$, $\sum_ix_i^{2}=\tfrac12$, $\sum_ix_i^{3}=0$, $\sum_p(N-p)n_p=n_1$, the double and triple sums of the
vortex numbers are empty, $\sum_{j}(N-j)n_j^{2}=n_1^{2}$ and $\sum_jn_j\sum_{i>j}x_i=\tfrac12n_1$.  Then
\[
A\varphi_0^{3}=2\varphi_0^{2}-12\pi^{2}b^{2}\bigl(\tfrac12-0\bigr)-24\pi^{2}b\bigl(0-\tfrac12n_1\bigr)-12\pi^{2}\bigl(n_1^{2}-\tfrac12n_1^{2}\bigr)
=2\varphi_0^{2}-6\pi^{2}(b-n_1)^{2},
\]
so the vortex enters only through the combination $b-n_1$ (a vortex in the loop shifts the flux by one flux quantum), $D=2/\varphi_0^{3}$, and
all four contributions to $R$ vanish: Term~2 vanishes because $\sum_kx_k=\sum_ix_i^{3}=0$; Term~7 gives
$24\pi^{3}b^{2}\bigl[\tfrac12n_1\cdot\tfrac12-n_1\cdot\tfrac14\bigr]=0$; Term~9 gives
$24\pi^{3}b\bigl[-n_1\cdot\tfrac12n_1+\tfrac12n_1^{2}\bigr]=0$; Term~3 gives
$8\pi^{3}\bigl[-\tfrac12n_1^{3}+\tfrac32n_1^{3}-n_1^{3}\bigr]=0$.  So $R=0$, as expected for two wires.  \checkmark

\emph{$N=3$.}  Take $x=(-\tfrac12,x_2,\tfrac12)$ and call $n_1=n_{12}$, $n_2=n_{23}$.  Then $\sum_kx_k=x_2$,
$\sum_ix_i^{2}=\tfrac12+x_2^{2}$, $\sum_p(N-p)n_p=2n_{12}+n_{23}$,
$\sum_jn_j\sum_{i>j}x_i=n_{12}(x_2+\tfrac12)+\tfrac12n_{23}$, and
$\sum_j(N-j)n_j^2+2\sum_{j<l}(N-l)n_jn_l=2n_{12}^{2}+2n_{12}n_{23}+n_{23}^{2}$.  Inserting,
\[
\varphi_0^{3}A=3\varphi_0^{2}-6\pi^{2}b^{2}-8\pi^{2}b^{2}x_2^{2}+12\pi^{2}b\,(n_{12}+n_{23})+8\pi^{2}b\,x_2\,(n_{12}-n_{23})-8\pi^{2}\bigl(n_{12}^{2}+n_{12}n_{23}+n_{23}^{2}\bigr),
\]
and the four contributions to $R$ are
\begin{align*}
\text{term 2:}&\quad\pi^{3}\bigl[\tfrac{16}{9}b^{3}x_2^{3}-4b^{3}x_2\bigr],\\
\text{term 7:}&\quad\pi^{3}\bigl[-\tfrac83b^{2}x_2^{2}(n_{12}-n_{23})+8b^{2}x_2(n_{12}+n_{23})+2b^{2}(n_{12}-n_{23})\bigr],\\
\text{term 9:}&\quad\pi^{3}\bigl[-\tfrac83b\,x_2(n_{12}^{2}+4n_{12}n_{23}+n_{23}^{2})-4b(n_{12}^{2}-n_{23}^{2})\bigr],\\
\text{term 3:}&\quad\pi^{3}\bigl[\tfrac{16}{9}n_{12}^{3}+\tfrac83n_{12}^{2}n_{23}-\tfrac83n_{12}n_{23}^{2}-\tfrac{16}{9}n_{23}^{3}\bigr].
\end{align*}
Their sum, together with $D=3/\varphi_0^{3}$, is the closed-form three-nanowire asymmetric result derived earlier in this
project,
$A=\bigl[3\varphi_0^{2}-3\pi^{2}\bigl(2b^{2}+\tfrac83b^{2}x_2^{2}-4b(n_{12}+n_{23})-\tfrac83b\,x_2(n_{12}-n_{23})+\tfrac83(n_{12}^{2}+n_{12}n_{23}+n_{23}^{2})\bigr)\bigr]/\varphi_0^{3}$,
and the same $R$.  \checkmark  (The term-2 line is the $b^{3}$ part already found in Parity check~2.  The general formulas for $A$, $D$, $R$ were also
compared with the direct wire-by-wire sum $\sum_i[\varphi_i/\varphi_0-(\varphi_i/\varphi_0)^{3}]$ in exact arithmetic for
